Moving around the image

Zoom matters most with the brush: a block is a few screen pixels at fit-to-window, and nothing can be aimed at what cannot be seen. The image stays pixelated at every zoom level — it is the thing being edited, not a preview of it.

Phone
Pinch with two fingers to zoom. Drag with two fingers, or with one while the brush is off, to move around.
Trackpad
Two-finger scroll moves the picture. Pinch to zoom — browsers report a trackpad pinch as ctrl and the wheel, which is how the two gestures are told apart.
Mouse
Ctrl and the wheel zooms under the cursor. The wheel on its own scrolls the picture. Holding the wheel down and pushing away zooms in, pulling back zooms out.
Panning
Drag with the right button at any time, or with the left button while no tool is on.
Getting back
Fit in the tool bar returns to the whole image.

The brush

It nudges, it does not paint
The brush shifts a block's value before the palette is matched, so what comes out is always a palette colour. A small nudge on a block far from a boundary shows nothing — it is still recorded, and it still counts towards the next one.
It nudges on arrival
A block is nudged the moment the brush moves onto it. Holding still adds nothing however long the press lasts, so the amount never depends on the speed of the drag — but sweeping back over an area is a second pass, and that is how a nudge is built up without lifting a finger. With a wide brush only the blocks it has just moved onto are nudged; the ones it is already sitting on are left alone.
Building up, and starting over
Rub back and forth over the same spot to push it further, one pass per crossing — turning around counts as leaving, so a wide brush builds up under its own middle rather than only along its edges. Passing twice with red at -4 puts that block at -8. It stops at 16 in either direction on each channel, however long the rub. Whatever it took, the whole press is a single undo step.
Seeing where a tap landed
A finger covers the block it is aiming at, so the outline of each stamp stays on the picture for a moment after the finger lifts. The length of that moment is under Brush marks in Settings; set it to 0 to turn it off.
Reach
On a phone a hand covers the thing being edited. Reach moves where the stroke lands relative to the finger, up to a quarter of the screen above or below, with a line showing the link. It is a share of the screen rather than a number of blocks, because a hand covers a fixed area of glass however far the picture is zoomed.
Sizes are in blocks
A size of 8 covers 8 blocks whatever the zoom, so the brush keeps its meaning at any zoom.
Undo is unlimited
Each stroke is one step. Changing the pixel size clears the brushwork, because the offsets were placed against a grid that no longer exists.

Drawing, and the divider

Brush vs drawing pen
The brush nudges a block and lets the palette matcher decide what that becomes. The drawing pen sets a block outright and takes the matcher out of the loop for those blocks. Only one can be on at a time.
The pen has nothing to build up
It covers every block it moves onto, and a block set to the colour it is already holding does not change — so going over the same ground twice looks the same as going over it once. The brush is the one where a second pass counts.
The line in a palette strip
Colours to the right of it were created for drawing. They are never matched to automatically — a colour mixed for one detail should not start attracting blocks across the whole image. They are placed by hand or not at all.
Adding colours in Palettes is different
Anything added on the Palettes screen goes before the divider and does take part in matching. That screen is for building the palette itself.
The eraser
Puts blocks back under the matcher rather than painting over them.

Smoothing away specks

Despeckle is the one to reach for
A clear sky often comes out with a few stray blocks in it — not clouds, just blocks whose average landed the other side of a palette boundary. Despeckle takes the median of each block's neighbours: an isolated speck is a minority in its own neighbourhood so it disappears, while an edge, with half its neighbours on each side, does not move.
Soften is a different job
A distance-weighted average. It reduces detail everywhere, edges included, and it smears a speck across its neighbourhood rather than removing it. Reach for it when losing detail is the point.
It runs before the palette
Which is what makes it work. After matching, every block is already one of a handful of colours, and averaging those gives colours the palette does not contain. Before matching, an almost-blue block is pulled the rest of the way and the matcher does what it would have done anyway.
Radius costs nothing for Soften
It is computed with a sliding window, so radius 20 costs what radius 2 costs. Despeckle is not separable and does grow with radius, which is why its radius is capped rather than merely discouraged.
Only part of the picture
Under Smooth, set Area above 1 x 1 to choose a filter, leave Strength at 0 so it does nothing globally, then turn on Paint the filter on and drag. The filter lands on the blocks painted and nowhere else, at full strength, and it is undoable like any other stroke.
Area first, or there is nothing to paint
At 1 x 1 the window is a single block, and a block averaged with itself is the same block — so there is no filter yet, for the whole picture or for the brush. The by-hand controls stay greyed until Area is set, rather than accepting strokes that could not change anything.

Cropping

It is measured in the photo's own pixels
Not in blocks. The block grid is recomputed over whatever is selected, so the Pixel size sliders keep their own numbers and get new limits.
Moving and resizing
Drag anywhere inside the selection to move it. Grab a corner, or the middle of any side, to resize. With a ratio locked, a corner follows whichever axis moved further and a side grows about the centre.
Shape and output size are different things
Locking 1:1 says the selection is square. Typing an output size of 1024 x 1024 says the result is that size — so a square can be dragged anywhere from small to the largest that fits and still land at 1024 x 1024. Leave the output blank to keep exactly the pixels selected.
Cropping twice
Turning crop back on brings the whole photo back with the last selection still on it. Crops are always taken from the original, never from a previous crop, so nothing compounds and the selection can always widen again.
Undo covers it
Moving the selection, resizing it, applying, and going back to the full image are all steps. Applying does clear the brush and pen history, because those offsets were placed on a grid that no longer exists.

Elsewhere

Video is sampled, not played
Every output frame is decoded at its own timestamp, so the result is exactly as long as the source and never freezes at the end. It takes longer than real time; that is the trade.
Palettes stay on the device
They live on this device. Transfer on the Palettes bar writes a file that moves to another one.

photo_to_pixels

Thank you

Pixelatte exists because another app got there first. photo_to_pixels was the one that made pixelating a photo feel like something worth doing rather than a filter to scroll past.

It was free. It did not (really) have ads. It worked. Nothing about it begged for a subscription, a login, or a rating out of five before you were allowed to use the thing you had just installed. You opened it, it did the job, you closed it.

That is a low bar in description and a rare one in practice, and it is the bar this app is trying to clear. The offline-by-default, no-account, nothing-leaves- your-phone shape of Pixelatte is not a privacy stance so much as an attempt to remember what that felt like.

So: thanks. This one is built in its shadow.

Account

There is no account to sign in to. Pixelatte runs entirely on this device: palettes, images and video never leave it, and nothing here needs a server. Maybe this will be implemented when the app launches. Until then, go grab a coffee instead of making accounts everywhere. Cheers!

For syncing palettes between devices, use Transfer on the Palettes bar — it writes a .json file that can be moved manually.

Smoothing

Spatial

Blocks are pulled towards their neighbours before the palette is matched, which is what removes stray specks — one white block in an otherwise clean sky, not a cloud. Runs on the block grid, so it costs the same whatever the video's resolution.

A row samples sideways only and a column downwards only, so each leaves streaks running its own way alone. At their shortest they are the 2x1 and 1x2 pairs.
